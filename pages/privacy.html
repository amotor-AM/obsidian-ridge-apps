Privacy policy

Effective 2026-11-09. Applies to Obsidian Ridge Labs apps on the Atlassian Marketplace and to this website.

Obsidian Ridge Labs ("we", "us") is an independent software studio in Las Vegas, Nevada, USA. This policy explains what our apps for Atlassian products read, what they keep, and what Atlassian shares with us. Questions go to support@obsidianridgelabs.com. Our iPhone and Mac apps have their own policy at obsidianridgelabs.com/privacy.

Read-only Gantt Chart for Jira

What it reads

When someone opens the chart, the app asks Jira, as that person, for the issues that match the chosen JQL query or saved filter. For each issue it reads the key, summary, issue type, status, assignee, parent, created date, the chosen start and end date fields, and issue links. It also reads the list of fields on the site and the person's starred filters. Jira only returns what that person is already allowed to see.

Where it goes

Nowhere outside Jira. The app runs in the viewer's browser inside Jira, on Atlassian's Forge platform. It has no server of its own and declares no external network access, and Forge blocks connections to any other address. We never receive, see or store the content of your Jira site.

What it keeps

The JQL query, zoom level and chosen date fields are remembered per project in the browser's local storage on the viewer's own device, so the chart opens the same way next time. They are not sent anywhere. Clearing site data for your Jira site in the browser removes them.

Exports

PNG and CSV exports are made in the browser and saved to the viewer's device. We do not receive them.

After uninstalling

We hold no data from your site, so there is nothing for us to delete. Settings in local storage stay in each browser until its site data is cleared.

Timesheet Report for Jira

What it reads

When someone opens the timesheet, the app asks Jira, as that person, for the issues with worklogs in the chosen period that match their JQL. For each issue it reads the key, summary, issue type, project, parent and worklogs (author, start time, time spent and comment). It also reads Jira's time tracking settings: hours per day, the time format, and whether time tracking is on. Jira only returns what that person is already allowed to see, including worklog visibility limits.

Where it goes

Nowhere outside Jira. The app runs in the viewer's browser inside Jira, on Atlassian's Forge platform. It has no server of its own and declares no external network access, and Forge blocks connections to any other address. We never receive, see or store the content of your Jira site.

What it keeps

The period, columns, grouping, JQL, Only my worklogs and hours format are remembered per page in the browser's local storage on the viewer's own device. Worklogs are not stored. Clearing site data for your Jira site in the browser removes the settings.

Exports

CSV exports are made in the browser and saved to the viewer's device. We do not receive them.

After uninstalling

We hold no data from your site, so there is nothing for us to delete. Settings in local storage stay in each browser until its site data is cleared.

Markdown Macro for Confluence

What it reads

Only the Markdown text saved in the macro on the page being viewed. Confluence passes it to the app when the page loads. The app asks for no Confluence permissions and cannot read other pages, spaces, users or attachments.

Where it goes

Nowhere outside Confluence. The Markdown is rendered in the viewer's browser on Atlassian's Forge platform. When a page is exported to PDF or Word, Confluence asks the app's export function, which runs on Forge, to convert the Markdown; it returns the result to Confluence and keeps nothing. The app declares no external network access, and Forge blocks connections to any other address. We never receive, see or store your page content.

What it keeps

Nothing. The Markdown is stored by Confluence as part of the page, with the page's own permissions and history. A .md file imported in the editor is read in the browser and not uploaded.

After uninstalling

We hold no data from your site, so there is nothing for us to delete. The Markdown stays in your pages as the macro's saved text.

Information Atlassian shares with us

When a customer installs or buys one of our apps, Atlassian gives us licensing and billing information as part of the Marketplace, for example the site address, the license type and size, and the names and email addresses of the license's technical and billing contacts. Atlassian also shows us totals such as how many sites have an app installed. We use this to provide support, manage licenses, and meet legal, tax and accounting duties. We keep it only as long as those purposes need or the law requires.

Support email

When you email us, we keep your message and email address to answer you and to look back at earlier questions from you. Do not send us confidential issue content; a description or a cropped screenshot is enough. You can ask us to delete your messages at any time.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent personal information, and we do not share it for advertising.
  • We do not use analytics, tracking or advertising tools in our apps.
  • We do not ask for passwords, API tokens or personal access tokens.

Atlassian's role

Atlassian hosts Jira, Confluence and the Forge platform our apps run on. Atlassian's processing of your data is covered by the Atlassian privacy policy and your agreement with Atlassian.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, including under the GDPR in Europe and the CCPA in California, you can ask what personal information we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, or object to how we use it. Email support@obsidianridgelabs.com and we will answer within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Our apps are business tools and are not directed at children under 16.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will update it on this page and change the effective date above. If a change affects what an app reads or keeps, we will say so in the app's release notes.
